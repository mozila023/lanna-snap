Chiang Rai (wiki): founded 1262 by Mangrai; aquatic: giant catfish; crops coffee, tea, pineapple (Nang Lae); Doi Tung 1,389 m; Golden Triangle; Phu Chi Fa NP.
Wat Phra Kaew Chiang Rai (wiki): 1434 lightning struck chedi → Emerald Buddha found under stucco.
Mangrai (wiki): b.1238/39; Chiang Rai 1262; Three Kings alliance 1287 (Ngam Mueang, Ramkhamhaeng); took Haripunchai 1291; Chiang Mai from 1296; died 1311/1317 (tradition: lightning in market).
Lamphun (wiki): Haripunchai, Mon; flower Flame of the Forest; aquatic chubby frog; famous longan; kuaitiao lamyai originated in Lamphun; Lamphun old wall; Khun Tan tunnel.
Camadevi (wiki): 7th–8th c.; raised by hermit Sudeva; invited c.659 to rule Haripunchai; sons Mahantayot & Anantayot; defeated Khun Luang Wilangka (Lawa ruler).
Kawila (wiki): b. Lampang 1742, grandson of Thipchang; ruled Chiang Mai 1782–1816; entered/restored Chiang Mai Feb 1797; took Chiang Saen 1804.
Tilokaraj (wiki): reign 1441–1487; built Wat Chet Yot; repelled Ayutthaya; conquered Nan & Phrae.
Nan (wiki): seal bull; motto: boat racing, black ivory, Wat Phumin paintings, golden oranges, Phra That Chae Haeng; Phukha dynasty; Doi Phu Kha NP; Bo Kluea = "salt well".
Black ivory (mgronline 9550000032734): sacred heirloom of Nan rulers, believed from Chiang Tung, kept at Nan National Museum (old Ho Kham).
Pu Man Ya Man (sanook 127389): mural at Wat Phumin by Nan Bua Phan, 1867–1874; "whisper of love".
Phrae (wiki): motto mor hom & teak, Phra Lo love story, Phra That Cho Hae, Phae Mueang Phi; Tham Pha Nang Khoi legend (woman waiting for lover turned to stone).
Phae Mueang Phi (wiki): erosion pillars, forest park est. 1981, "ghost town grove", 8 km NE of Phrae.
Phayao (wiki): Kwan Phayao lake largest freshwater fish habitat upper north; aquatic Mekong giant catfish; Phra Chao Ton Luang ~18 m tall, lap 16 m; Doi Phu Nang peafowl; water hyacinth crafts; Thai Lue cotton.
Ngam Mueang (wiki): ruled Phayao from 1258; friend of Ramkhamhaeng; Three Kings alliance; "sweet curry" story; name "shades over the land" (weather legend).
Mae Hong Son (wiki): elephant camp early 19th c. by Lord Kaeo of Ma; "city of three mists"; flower bua tong; animal Blyth's river frog; Doi Kong Mu; Pang Oung; Mae Surin Falls; bua tong fields Doi Mae U-Kho; Poi Sang Long.
Uttaradit (wiki): seal Wat Phra Thaen Sila At; tree teak; motto Namphi metal, langsat, Phraya Phichai Dap Hak; durian Longlaplae/Linlaplae (odourless); Sirikit Dam; Ton Sak Yai (world's largest teak, ~1,500 yr).
Phraya Phichai (wiki): b.1741; under Taksin; broke a sword in battle 1773 at Phichai; executed 1782 at 41 by his own request.
Laplae legend (sanook 108313): man enters a village of women, must never lie; lies to comfort son, sent away with turmeric that turns to gold.
Doi Inthanon (wiki): 2,565 m; named after Inthawichayanon, Grand Prince of Chiang Mai, who preserved forests; −5 °C record 2017.
Bo Sang (wiki): Tai Lue village; handmade painted umbrellas; festival 3rd week of January.
Khun Tan tunnel (wiki): 1,352.1 m, finished 1918; 2nd longest since 2024.
Lampang: see /home/claude/lampang/RESEARCH.md

## Set 2 (2026-11-04): legends, spirits, rituals, myths, festivals, crafts, food
- Dara Rasmi: royal consort of Rama V, daughter of Inthawichayanon, b. 1873; promoted Lanna farming, dance, weaving; Suan Chao Sabai, Mae Rim. Sources: silpa-mag.com/?p=35858, chula.ac.th/news/129672
- Chiraprapha: queen regnant c. 1545–1546, negotiated with Ayutthaya; Wisutthithewi: ruler of Chiang Mai under Burmese rule c. 1564–1578, last of the Mangrai line. Sources: matichon weekly (?p=907832), silpa-mag.com/?p=98141
- Phraya Singhanat Racha: Shan leader, first governor of Mae Hong Son, c. 1874. Source: lovethailand.org memorial page, gotoknow 409011
- Chao Anantaworarittidet: ruler of Nan 1852–1891, restored Wat Phumin (Pu Man Ya Man mural). Source: silpa-mag (Nan tag), matichon prachachuen 4156638
- Chao Phiriya Thepphawong: last ruler of Phrae; 1902 Shan rebellion; exile to Luang Prabang. Source: silpa-mag.com/?p=95717, ?p=139370
- Pu Sae Ya Sae & Liang Dong at Doi Kham: silpa-mag.com/culture/article_154100, so05.tci-thaijo.org sjss 20381
- Spirits (phi suea ban/muang, phi pu ya, phi ka, phi pong, phi khun nam, phi mot phi meng), rituals (suep chata, alms, bathing stupa, tung offering, song khro), myths (naga, singha, Erawan, hamsa, kinnaree, mom), festivals (Yi Peng, Poy Sang Long, Nan boat race, Tan Kuay Salak, Pi Mai Mueang): general Lanna folklore; card text kept descriptive and respectful. No monks or Buddha images depicted.
- Places: Doi Luang Chiang Dao (Chao Luang Kham Daeng legend), Pai, Tha Phae Sunday walking street, Bo Kluea salt wells (Nan), Phu Langka NP (Phayao), Chae Son hot springs (Lampang).
